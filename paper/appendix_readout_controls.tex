\section{Readout and inverse-dynamics controls}
\label{app:ablations}

We train randomly initialized ViT-tiny world models on 10,000 successful
trajectories for ten epochs, with batch size 128 and seed 3072. The primary
Color, Shape, and Palette models combine a learned spatial readout with an auxiliary
inverse-dynamics loss (IDM) of weight 3.125. Both are adaptations of LeWM:
the native recipe selects the CLS token and uses prediction MSE plus SIGReg,
without IDM. The following one-seed comparisons examine how these choices
affect planning, tunnel localization, and cue accessibility.

IDM predicts the actions connecting consecutive states, encouraging features
that distinguish their effects. Related uses of inverse dynamics emphasize
controllable features \citep{ivashkov2026sensorimotor}, while auxiliary-task
analyses examine how additional supervision can preserve distinctions missed
by prediction alone \citep{yu2025auxjepa}. Our IDM provides action supervision,
but no direct supervision of portal identity or cue--destination binding.
Spatial aggregation instead preserves patch position before compressing the
visual features into a single vector. The controls below assess whether
either choice accounts for the observed location--cue dissociation.

For the primary models, the objective is
\[
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pred}}+.09\mathcal L_{\mathrm{SIGReg}}
 +\frac{3.125}{10}\,\mathbb E\|h([z_{t+1},z_t])-a_t\|_2^2.
\]
Here $a_t$ contains five normalized two-dimensional primitive actions;
the IDM MSE averages over all ten coordinates, examples, and time steps.
The auxiliary head concatenates the next and current projected states, in
that order, and applies
Linear$(384,512)$, LayerNorm, GELU, and Linear$(512,10)$. Its loss propagates
through both states; the action target is detached. This head is used only
during training and differs from the inverse model in Sensorimotor World
Models \citep{ivashkov2026sensorimotor}.

\subsection{Why include inverse dynamics and a spatial readout?}

IDM provides a substantial planning benefit in the historical Color
comparison (Table~\ref{tab:idmplanning}). With the spatial readout, success
across the 50 cases rises from 8\% without IDM to 48\% with IDM for CEM,
and from 8\% to 58\% for GD. Both ranks improve, and CLS shows the same
direction of change. Each IDM pair shares its initial model parameters,
training budget, and evaluation protocol. These gains motivate including
IDM in the primary configuration, while its remaining failures on the
farther correct tunnel preserve the cue-binding challenge.

Spatial aggregation serves a complementary purpose: making tunnel positions
more accessible (Table~\ref{tab:historicalreadout}). At IDM weight 3.125,
location MAE is 3.83 pixels with the spatial readout versus 7.42 with CLS,
and location $R^2$ is .934 versus .684. The spatial model also localizes
well without IDM ($R^2=.898$), despite its poor planning performance.
Accurate localization alone is therefore insufficient for reliable planning
in these runs. This motivates the combination used in the main study.
The readout comparison uses the same seed but does not share encoder
initialization, so it is less tightly controlled than each IDM pair.

Neither addition resolves cue binding. With IDM, both readouts have cue
MAE near 27 pixels; the spatial state's cue $R^2$ is .224, versus .307
for CLS. Without IDM, the spatial state's cue $R^2$ is only .162 despite
its strong localization. Thus IDM is not required for the selective
location--cue gap to appear, and spatial aggregation does not by itself
restore cue identity. These one-seed results support our configuration
choices without establishing that either adaptation is universally necessary.

\subsection{Current and predicted spatial-state probes}

The location--cue gap appears in both current projected states and one-step
predictions (Table~\ref{tab:spatialfull}). The primary Color, Shape, and Palette models
share the readout, IDM weight, training budget, and probe protocol, although
their source revisions differ. We obtained Shape's positional scores from
retained frozen features and aligned sidecar targets using the original ridge
procedure, which also reproduces its archived cue scores.

Palette uses a later source revision with the same nominal training recipe.
Its final checkpoint yields current location $R^2=.930$ and cue $R^2=.258$;
one-step predictions retain the gap. The source difference means this is
not a fully controlled palette-only intervention.

\subsection{Matched CLS controls across three cue renderings}

A separate experiment compares all three cue renderings with native CLS
selection, with and without IDM of weight 3.125
(Table~\ref{tab:matchedidm}). These six conditions share one source revision
and are distinct from the primary spatial checkpoints. Within each pair,
the data interface, ten-epoch budget, seed 3072, and initialization of shared
modules are matched; adding the IDM head does not change that initialization.

All three CLS models without IDM have negative location and cue $R^2$.
IDM improves both, with a larger gain for location. Weak cue accessibility
therefore recurs across these conditions, but selective preservation of
location does not extend to the native CLS/no-IDM controls. Together with
Table~\ref{tab:historicalreadout}, this comparison shows that readout and
objective affect the observed failure. It neither isolates a unique cause
nor establishes that LeWM generally requires IDM.

The three CLS+IDM models also produce mixed or faint cue markers in their
reconstructions (Figure~\ref{fig:clscontroldecoders}). Their pixel decoders

These qualitative results concern the separate CLS controls. Fixed walls
can be reconstructed without recovering variable portal geometry, and the
images alone do not quantify cue identity.

\section{The spatial patch aggregator}
\label{app:aggregator}

The spatial readout is inspired by the learned aggregation of visual
features in \emph{Temporal Straightening for Latent Planning}
\citep{wang2026temporal}. That work studies convolutional projectors and a
separate MLP aggregation head for spatial-feature straightening. Our readout
uses a pooled patch grid to construct the feature supplied to LeWM, without
the temporal-curvature regularizer.

The primary spatial readout consumes patch tokens only; the native CLS token
is discarded. For a square ViT patch grid, it reshapes tokens from
$B\times P\times D$ to $B\times D\times\sqrt P\times\sqrt P$, applies
adaptive average pooling to $8\times8$, flattens the resulting $64D$ values,
then applies LayerNorm and a learned linear map to a 192-dimensional global
feature. The same aggregator processes each observation in a temporal
window; CLS controls select token zero directly. In both cases, the LeWM
projector applies Linear$(192,2048)$, BatchNorm, GELU, and
Linear$(2048,192)$ to obtain the state used for prediction and planning.
The probe labeled ``current'' acts on this projected state, rather than on
raw encoder tokens or the output of the aggregator alone.
